This survey collects {n} papers that frame the V3 project: a timing-predictable INT8 CNN accelerator on the AMD Kria KV260. In V3 the latency of an inference is known exactly before it runs, because the cycle model, the RTL simulation and the board agree cycle for cycle. Reconfigurable dataflow (split-K, depthwise mode, per-layer mode selection) and sparsity skipping are meant to make it faster without losing that guarantee. The papers are grouped into seven categories: {categories}.

Method. One search was run per category, preferring peer-reviewed journal and conference papers from the last ten years plus key classics. Papers already checked in the project's earlier novelty review were reused where they fit. Every paper was verified by script: its DOI must resolve at doi.org, and the title, all authors, venue, volume, issue, pages and year in this table are copied from the Crossref record (DataCite or arXiv where the paper has no Crossref record). The year is the print year when Crossref has one. "n/a" marks a field that the record does not have, for example the issue number of a conference paper.

The columns "Work done", "Advantages" and "Disadvantages" summarise what the paper itself states. The basis of each summary is given in the table: {n_full} papers were summarised from the full text and {n_abs} from the abstract. Disadvantages are limits stated by the authors or clear scope limits (device, network, dataset, precision, simulation-only evaluation), worded neutrally. "Relevance" is a one-line note on how the paper relates to V3.

Limitations. This is a curated narrative survey, not a systematic review. Papers were selected for relevance to V3, and the category counts were set in advance. Summaries based only on the abstract do not assess a paper's methods. The search, the summaries and the document were prepared with AI assistance (Claude Code subagents and scripts) and checked against the sources as described above. The machine-readable data are in survey50.csv and survey50.bib.
