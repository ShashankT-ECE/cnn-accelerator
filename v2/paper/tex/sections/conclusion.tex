\section{Conclusion and Limitations}\label{sec:conclusion}
A small INT8 accelerator can be predictable by construction. On the KV260 our
$8{\times}8$ output-stationary design matches its analytical cycle model
exactly on every layer, in RTL simulation and on the board, over 20,000 test
images and 286 random layer jobs; its logits are bit-exact; and its compute
latency of 65.7\,$\mu$s (LeNet-5) and 417.0\,$\mu$s (CIFAR-10) did not vary by
a single cycle. It is 2.1--3.1$\times$ faster end to end than the best CPU
baseline on the same board, beats the AMD DPU on LeNet-5, and loses to it on
CIFAR-10.

The work has clear limits. The networks are small, and only VALID
convolutions with stride~1 and kernel width up to 9 are supported. A single
$8{\times}8$ array does useful work in only 39.6\,\% of its PE-cycles on LeNet-5. SOM-rail
power includes the CPU that drives the accelerator, so it is not
accelerator-only power. End-to-end latency depends on a Python host. Future
work adds padding and stride, a larger array (a 16$\times$16 array is only
projected by the model so far) and larger networks.
