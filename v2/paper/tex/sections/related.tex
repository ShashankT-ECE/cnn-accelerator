\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}
\textbf{FPGA CNN accelerators.} Output-, weight- and row-stationary dataflows
trade data reuse against utilization~\cite{sze2017efficient}. On FPGAs,
roofline-guided loop tiling~\cite{zhang2015optimizing} and quantitative
loop-optimization analysis~\cite{ma2018optimizing} choose a design from a
model and confirm it by implementation. FINN~\cite{umuroglu2017finn} builds
per-layer streaming pipelines for binarized networks on the same small
benchmarks we use. Angel-Eye~\cite{guo2018angeleye} maps CNNs onto embedded
Zynq devices through a compiler and an instruction-driven engine. Our
numeric contract follows integer-only quantization with a fixed-point
multiplier and shift~\cite{jacob2018quantization}.

\textbf{Performance models.} Timeloop~\cite{parashar2019timeloop} and
MAESTRO~\cite{kwon2019maestro} project performance and energy across large
design spaces, and SCALE-Sim~\cite{samajdar2020scalesim} simulates systolic
arrays cycle by cycle. They are built for breadth across designs. Gemmini~\cite{genc2021gemmini} shows that
system effects outside the accelerator change measured results, which is
also what separates our compute and end-to-end latencies.

\textbf{Vendor IP.} The AMD DPU~\cite{amd_pg338} is the standard way to run
CNNs on Zynq UltraScale+ and Kria devices; recent work characterizes its
multithreaded throughput on the KV260~\cite{costa2026kv260}. We use it as a
same-board baseline.

Few of these works report an analytical model, RTL simulation and board
measurement that agree exactly, layer by layer; that agreement is the focus
of this paper.
